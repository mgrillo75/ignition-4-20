1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This requirements document defines a holistic Power Plant Control System for VoltaGrid's typical Data Center Power
Plant design. This document encompasses the full ecosystem of control: telemetric sensors, IEDs, electromechanical
controls, manual backup control devices, Master Controllers, HMIs, Data processing servers/applications,
operational networks, and integration strategies for AUX systems. This is in addition to a traditional PMS
specification, that focuses only on master controllers and HMIs.

The intent is not for any single supplier to be responsible for the entire scope - instead this specification establishes
the baseline requirements that should guide separate scopes of work across OEMs, EPCs, integrators, and internal
VoltaGrid teams (including a PMS supplier) to ensure system components can integrate seamlessly into a unified,
resilient plant control architecture.

Not all components of this specification will apply to every VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant ­ each plant will
include different makes, models, and quantities of generators and DERs as needed to satisfy the customer
requirements. Additionally, the type and integration requirements for AUX systems, backup systems, customer
control system interfaces, backup/utility power system connections, etc. may vary from plant to plant. The sections
of this specification related to these systems are intended to communicate VoltaGrid's high-level philosophical
strategy for integrating these systems ­ not as prescriptive requirements for a single power plant.

1.1. PHILOSOPHY                                                                         Revision:        Page 9

This specification focuses on three fundamental priorities:                                  Page:

Reliability and Resilience ­ The plant shall satisfy a minimum of 99.9% availability and shall be concurrently
serviceable. In the event of a failure the plant shall remain operable, even if the control room or central automation
layer is completely disabled. Individual gensets, DERs, breakers, and AUX systems shall be fully operable through
their local IEDs and/or hardwired manual control devices. This ensures operators can still start, stop, and regulate
equipment in the field even in a complete loss of remote control/automation.

No single point of failure - whether a master controller, HMI, server, generator, switch, conductor, etc. - should result
in a plant-wide blackout. Furthermore, the plant should remain online and operable even in the event of a complete
control room shut down (removing power from all redundant Master Controllers, HMIs, Data Aggregators, Network
Switches, etc.). When controls/communications are reestablished, after an update, maintenance item, or failure,
the control system shall reassume control without causing any equipment shutdowns, transient/step changes in any
set points, or changes in operator-selected mode selections (Block, Auto, etc.).

Modular and Decoupled Design ­ Each subsystem (genset packages, electrical protection relays, gas distribution,
SCR/urea systems, AUX MCCs, etc.) is required to operate independently, with open (non-proprietary, industry
standard, and ethernet-based) communication protocols (Modbus, IEC 61850, IEEE C37.118, DNP3, MQTT).

This prevents vendor lock-in, simplifies upgrades, and enables straightforward integration of new assets such as
BESS, new generator makes/models, or alternative DERs such as synchronous condensers.

Operator Accessibility ­ Operators shall always have multiple levels of control to ensure continuous plant
operations:

1. Automatic control via Master Controllers
2. Remote control via HMIs, IEDs, and embedded subsystems
3. Manual Backup Control through switches, pushbuttons, and hand-operated valves

Master Controllers, HMIs, Data Aggregators, and Network architecture are required to be redundant to prevent loss
of operator situational awareness.

1.2. SCOPE

The document covers requirements across the entire plant control system stack as shown in Figure 1:
     · Plant Control System Objectives: Performance, operability, functionality, capability, and reliability targets
          for the Plant Control System.
     · Telemetric Devices: Sensors for pressure, temperature, vibration, flow, level, voltage, and current across
          gensets, distribution, gas, emissions, and AUX systems.
     · Electromechanical Control Devices: Governors, exciters, breakers, contactors, valves, and drives, each with
          defined IED control interfaces.
     · Manual Backup Controls: Local start/stop, selector switches, breaker operation switches, and emergency
          shutdown systems to guarantee control independent of automation layers.
     · IEDs and Controllers: Genset controllers, engine controllers, electrical protection relays, AUX system
          controllers, and DER controllers - responsible for local machine-level automation while exposing all relevant
          data to the Plant Network and Cluster.
     · Network Infrastructure: Redundant Ethernet-based communications backbone, supporting Modbus, IEC
          61850, IEC C37.118, DNP, MQTT, etc.
     · Master Controllers: Redundant supervisory controllers responsible for system-wide coordination (black
          start, bus transfer, load sharing, reserve margin management, emissions compliance). Failover logic ensures
          operations continue in droop mode if both controllers are lost.
     · HMIs and Data Aggregators: Operator visibility into real-time status, alarms, trending data, and control
          functions. Fully redundant, with integration into cloud storage.
     · Gateway Servers, Historians, and Data Storage: Long term data storage and view port population.
     · Test Reports: RTDS Simulations, HIL FAT, and SAT

1.3. KEY FEATURES

     · Load Management: Real and reactive power load sharing, reserve margin management, and minimum
          genset loading enforcement.

     · Failure Mode Resilience: Loss of any single component (controller, HMI, network switch, aggregator) must
          not result in a system-wide blackout. Failures default to safe alarms, limp-mode, or limited unit shutdown
          rather than cascading outage.

     · Black Start & Dead Bus Arbitration: Automated recovery sequences for plant restart following complete
          outage.

     · Fuel & Emissions Control: Automated control of gas distribution and emissions treatment systems (SCR,
          bulk urea) independent of Master Controllers.

     · Testing & Documentation: RTDS Simulation, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and SAT will validate all
          requirements. Each OEM/Supplier must provide cause-and-effect matrices, wiring diagrams,
          communication maps, and sequence of operation documentation to ensure consistency with the
          requirements defined here.

1.4. STRATEGIC VALUE

     · Resilient Operations: Manual backup controls and robust controller redundancy ensures that the
          operator(s) can maintain power delivery in the field without reliance on the control room, aligning with
          VoltaGrid's ethos of toughness, practicality, and operational reliability.

     · Future-Proofed Architecture: Modular, protocol-based design enables easy adoption of new technologies
          and DERs.

     · Integration Flexibility: Requirements allow VoltaGrid to competitively source components without
          dependency on a single OEM, while ensuring compatibility.

1.5. CONCLUSION

This requirements specification defines a resilient, flexible, and operator-friendly Plant Control System tailored to
VoltaGrid's mission-critical data center operations. By expanding beyond a narrow PMS approach, it ensures the
system is robust against failures, accessible at multiple levels, and modular enough to integrate future technologies.
For VoltaGrid and its customers, this means assured uptime, simplified integration, and confidence that the plant
remains under control even under worst-case conditions.

2. INTRODUCTION

To achieve safe, effective, efficient, reliable, and recordable operations; All modern power plants must include a
robust and reliable Plant Control System. A modern Plant Control System typically consists of and/or relies on the
following items:

     · Telemetric devices including but not limited to:
                o Pressure sensors
                o Temperature Sensors
                o Status contacts
                o PTs
                o CTs

     · Electromechanical control devices including but not limited to:
                o Governors (or pre-chamber gas valves)
                o Exciters
                o Trip Coils
                o Close Coils
                o Control Valves
                o Contactors
                o Visual indicators

     · Manual control devices including but not limited to:
                o Buttons
                o Switches
                o Hand-operated valves

     · IEDs including but not limited to:
                o Engine Controllers
                o Genset Controllers
                o DER Controllers for assets such as:
                            Synchronous Condensers
                            BESS
                o AUX system Controllers for systems including but not limited to:
                            SCRs
                            Bulk Urea Distribution System
                            Fuel distribution
                o Electrical Protection Relays

3. OVERVIEW

A typical VoltaGrid Power Plant Control System Overview is shown below in Figure 2. In general, IEDs and/or the
Master Controller actuate/modulate electromechanical control devices based on inputs/information from
telemetric devices, manual control devices, and/or the operator (via the Control Room HMI). Meanwhile, the Data
Aggregator collects and retransmits telemetric data and/or control inputs to the Control Room HMI, Time-Series
Trending Application, and Gateway Server. The Control Room HMI provides the operator with limited control inputs
as well as the data visualizations that are required for adequate situational awareness.

The "Machinery" that makes up a VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant is divided into eight categories:
     · MV Gensets
     · DER Systems
     · MV Electrical Distribution
     · LV Electrical Distribution System
     · Backup LV Gensets
     · Fuel Gas Distribution System
     · Bulk Urea Distribution System
     · SCRs

Figure 3 shows a typical site layout for these assets. In this layout, piping from the Gas Yard and Bulk Urea Yard
distributes fuel gas and Urea to the J620 (right) and J624 (left) gensets and their associated SCRs. This allows the
engines to run in compliance with emissions regulations and produce mechanical power. The engines run at 1500
RPM and are coupled to their associated alternators through gear boxes allowing their 4-pole rotors to rotate at
1800RPM ­ producing 60Hz electric power at the stators.

Local genset excitation control systems maintain the stator voltage at a nominal 13.8kV phase-to-phase. The genset
stators are then coupled to the main 34.5kV buses through GCBs, GSUs, and ICBs. The GCBs are the synchronizing
breakers, and the Gensets' 480V AUX XFMRs are down stream of the GCBs ­ This allows the plant Operator to close
the ICB and energize the gensets' AUX systems so long as the main bus is energized.

The Main 34.5kV Bus is also coupled to Site AUX XFMR ICBs, an ABB AMS 1400 Synchronous Condenser ICB, and the
customer FCBs. The site AUX XFMRs supply power to the Site LV E-House which distributes power back to the Gas
Yard and Urea Yard as well as all other site-level AUX loads (Sync Con E-House, Ops Building, WIC, Site Lighting, Etc.).

The load side of the Customer FCBs is the point of interconnection with the customer's MV electrical distribution
system.

5. DEFINITIONS AND ABBREVIATIONS

The following definitions are applicable to this document:
     · Alternating Current (AC) ­ A type of electrical current where the direction of electron flow reverses back
          and forth at regular intervals, typically creating a sine wave pattern.

· Alternator ­ A machine including a rotor, stator, and excitation system which receives mechanical energy
     from an Engine, converts it to electrical energy, and transmits it to the main bus via terminations, bussing,
     cables, breakers, XFMRs, and SWGR.

· Application Programming Interface (API) ­ A set of rules and protocols that allows two different software
     programs to communicate with each other and exchange data.

· Auxiliary (AUX) ­ Secondary, supplementary, complementary, or additional.

· Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) ­ An intelligent electrical device that automatically detects a power
     outage from the main power source and instantly switches electrical load to a backup power source
     ensuring seamless, uninterrupted power, then switches back when the primary power source returns, all
     without human intervention.

· Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) ­ An electronic device that maintains a steady, constant voltage level
     for electrical equipment, regardless of fluctuations in the input power source, by automatically sensing
     changes and adjusting the output to prevent damaging sags, surges, or spikes, ensuring reliable power for
     sensitive electronics, generators, and industrial systems.

· Balance of Plant (BOP) ­ All AUX components, systems, and infrastructure essential for a power plant or
     industrial facility to operate, excluding the primary energy conversion equipment (like generators or
     turbines).

· Balance of Plant Management System (BOPMS) ­ The portion of the Control Room HMI which allows the
     Operator to monitor the status of all BOP equipment and distribution telemetry. If a single redundant BOP
     device has failed, the Operator should be able to see the revised system flows/states from the BOPMS.

     electromechanical control devices and other Intelligent Electronic Control Devices.

· Current Transformer (CT) ­ An instrument transformer that "steps down" high AC currents in power
     systems to a lower, safer, and standardized level (like 5A or 1A) for measurement by meters, relays, and
     monitoring devices.

· Distributed Energy Resource (DER) ­ Any machine, not including Gensets, which is connected via an
     alternator or inverter to the main bus. These include but are not limited to generators, synchronous
     condensers, BESS, Ultracapacitors, STATCOMs, etc.

· Direct Current (DC) ­ The unidirectional flow of electric charge, meaning the current moves consistently in
     one direction, typically from a negative to a positive pole. Voltage is typically static in DC systems.

6. PLANT CONTROL SYSTEM OBJECTIVES

The following sections define the objectives that the here-defined Plant Control System requirements are intended
to achieve.

6.1. GENERATOR AND DER START/STOP

The Plant Control System shall provide means to start and stop generators/DERs locally, remotely (via the Control
Room HMI), and automatically depending on site/plant conditions ­ To including start/stop control for the backup
diesel generator for emergency AUX power.

6.2. GENERATOR AND DER LOCAL AND REMOTE MODE SELECTION

The Plant Control System shall provide the Operator with the ability to block start and control the operating mode
(manual, auto, local, remote) for each generator/DER both from the local HMI/control panel and from the Control
Room HMI.

6.3. GENERATOR AND DER SYNCHRONIZATION

If the primary or secondary common bus is energized prior to an Engine start sequence and the machine's ICB is
closed, the Plant Control System shall automatically synchronize the machine to the common bus and then close the
GCB. For gensets, this function should be performed by the Genset Controller.

If the machine's ICB is open, and the Main bus is energized, the control system should block close on the associated
Genset GCB.

6.4. FREQUENCY/VOLTAGE REGULATION

The Plant Control System shall attempt to maintain generator, common bus, and customer feeder breaker voltages
and frequencies at nominal values (13.8kV or 34.5kV and 60Hz). During and after transient events, the control system
shall endeavor to restore nominal voltages and frequencies as quickly as possible.

VoltaGrid's frequency/voltage stability targets are as follows:
     · Transient frequency dips at the Main Bus shall be no less than 58.2 Hz (97% of nominal)
     · Transient Frequency swells at the Main Bus shall be no greater than 61.2 Hz (102% of nominal)
     · Transient Phase-Phase Voltage dips at the Main Bus shall be no less than 32.8kV (95% of nominal)
     · Transient Phase-Phase Voltage swells at the Main Bus shall be no greater than 36.2kV (105% of nominal)

VoltaGrid will design/select/add DERs in the MV system to ensure the above is physically possible, however, the
Master Controller, Genset Controllers, and DER Controllers shall be capable of dispatching these resources as needed
to meet the requirement.

6.5. GENERATOR AND DER LOAD SHARING

The Plant Control System shall utilize speed and voltage bias signals generated by the Master Control System to
balance the real and reactive power loads across all generators and to utilize DERs to minimize voltage/frequency
disturbances as much as possible.

6.6. MEDIUM VOLTAGE BREAKER OPEN/CLOSE

The Plant Control System shall provide means to open and close all MV breakers and disconnect switches locally,
remotely, and automatically depending on site/plant conditions.

Note: The operator should not be able to initiate a GCB closure except by initiating an Engine start sequence, after
which the Genset Controller and generator protection relay will automatically synchronize the Genset to the main
bus and close the GCB.

The Plant Control System shall provide the Master Controller and Operator (via the local HMI or Control Room HMI)
with the ability to block close for each breaker independently.

6.7. MANUAL BACKUP CONTROL

In General, the Plant Control System should include sufficient Manual Backup Control mechanisms such that the
Operator can still control the plant in the event of a complete Control Room shutdown including a loss of power to
both Master Controllers and the VoltaGrid Cluster.

For all failures which do not present a safety issue for personnel, or risk of equipment damage, and that do prevent
the operator from starting/stopping generators or opening/closing breakers through the normal HMI interfaces; The
Plant Control System shall provide a backup, manual, means of generator start/stop and breaker open/close control.

For generators, there should be local run/stop buttons and mode selector switches on the local Genset control panel
or local Genset Controller HMI. For breakers, excluding GCBs, the Plant Control System shall include remote
operation switches (Chicken Switches) which may be manually operated to open and close the breaker (provided
that a block close is not asserted).

6.8. ELECTRICAL PROTECTION

The Plant Control System shall make every attempt possible to prevent injury to personnel, damage to equipment,
and system -wide blackouts during electrical failures. Electrical protection relay settings should be developed in
accordance with all applicable equipment data sheets/ratings, Arc Flash and Coordination Studies, customer
requirements, and standard industry practices.

Note: GOOSE-based GSU Differential Protection and Breaker Failure Protection shall be provided by the GCB and ICB
Electrical Protection Relays.

6.9. EQUIPMENT PROTECTION

The Plant Control System should automatically execute the necessary control sequences required to ensure that

initiated, the Master Controller should systematically start/synchronize generators and open/close SWGR in order
to restore the plant to normal operations after a black out event.

The status of the Black Start control sequence shall be provided to the operator via the Control Room HMI.

If required, the Plant Control System should be capable of Load Shedding (opening specific breakers to maintain

6.13. GENSET PRIORITY MANAGEMENT

The Plant Control System shall provide the Operator with the means to assign a priority level to each genset in the
plant (via the Control Room HMI). There should be 100 priority levels ­ ensuring the Operator can assign a different
priority level to every Genset if desired. The Operator should also be able to assign the same priority level to multiple
Gensets if desired.

These priority levels will determine which Genset the Master Controller will automatically start/stop for IRM and
Minimum Genset Load Management.

6.14. INSTANTANEOUS RESERVE MARGIN MANAGEMENT

The control system shall automatically start, synchronize, and close-in additional Gensets to the common bus (so
long as at least one Genset is stopped and set to start automatically) as required to maintain an instantaneous
reserve margin that satisfies the customer needs according to anticipated load profiles.

When a generator start is initiated to satisfy IRM requirements, two generators should be started and synchronized
to the common bus. If both generators succeed in their start sequences and synchronize to the common bus, then
the generator with the lowest priority should be unloaded and shut down.

If neither generator succeeds in its start sequence and synchronization, then (if available) two more generators
should attempt to start and synchronize to the common bus.

All pertinent IRM Management statuses shall be provided to the operator via the Control Room HMI.

6.15. MINIMUM GENSET LOAD MANAGEMENT

The control system shall automatically unload generators and open their GCBs as necessary in order to maintain a
minimum load on all running/synchronized generators. If IRM and minimum loading requirements are in conflict,
IRM requirements shall take priority, and no generators shall be unloaded/disconnected from the common bus.

All pertinent Minimum Load Management statuses shall be provided to the operator via the Control Room HMI.

6.27. RELIABILITY

In General, every effort should be made to prevent a single point of failure. Single device failures shall not result in
system-wide blackouts, and efforts shall be made to duplicate signals where necessary to prevent reduced remote
control capabilities and/or loss of data in HMI(s), Time-Series Trending Application(s), and Gateway Server(s).

Wherever possible, single device failures should only result in an alarm. When possible, a failure of two (both)
redundant devices should result in a loss of visibility, limp-mode operations, or a single unit out of N+2 shutdown
but not a system-wide blackout.

For mechanical device failures which do not present a safety concern, the device should fail in an operational state
(breaker closed/valve open).

For mechanical device failures which do present a safety concern, the device should fail into a non-operational/safe
state (breaker open/valve closed).

The Plant Control System shall not infringe upon the Power Plant's ability to maintain a 99.9% up-time.

Any required maintenance items shall allow for concurrent serviceability. Maintenance items shall not require a
system-wide blackout.

6.28. ALARM/ALERT MANAGEMENT

Cluster/Message Bus.

7.2.1. Engine Starters
Engine Starter Motors shall be remotely actuated by the Engine and/or Genset Controllers when requested by the
Operator (via the Control Room HMI or a Manual Control Device) or the Master Controller provided that the Genset
is in AUTO and no Block Start is condition is present.

The Engine Starter Motor control signal type shall be defined by the Engine OEM. Engine and/or Genset Controller
start/stop signal values shall be exposed to the OT network via an Open Source Ethernet Protocol.

7.2.2. Engine Governors
Engine Governors are critical electromechanical control devices in VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plants as they are
the primary means of controlling Genset real power output levels and maintaining the system frequency. The Genset
governors shall receive throttle position/speed control signals from the Engine Controller.

The Engine/Genset Controller shall run the governor in droop by default but accept speed control biases from the
Master Controller (or from an IED retransmitting Master Controller control signals) to maintain the Main Bus at 60Hz
for all load levels. During normal operations the Master Controller will run the Gensets in a communications-assisted
Droop-Sharing mode for real power.

The Governor control signal type/range shall be defined by the Engine OEM. Engine and/or Genset Controller
governor control signal values shall be exposed to the OT network via an Open Source Ethernet Protocol.

7.2.3. Alternator Exciters
Alternator Exciters are also critical electromechanical control devices in VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plants as they
are the primary means of controlling Genset reactive power output levels and maintaining the system voltage. The
alternator excitation control systems receive voltage control signals either directly from the Genset Controller or
indirectly from the Genset Controller via an intermediary excitation Controller or AVR.

The Genset Controller or AVR shall run the Alternator in droop by default but accept voltage control biases from the
Master Controller (or from an IED retransmitting Master Controller control signals) to maintain the Main Bus at
34.5kV for all load levels. During normal operations the Master Controller will run the Gensets in a communications-
assisted Droop-Sharing mode for reactive power.

The excitation control system signal type/range shall be defined by the Engine OEM. Genset Controller and/or AVR
excitation control signal values shall be exposed to the OT network via an Open Source Ethernet Protocol.

8. DISTRIBUTED ENERGY RESOURCES

A DER is any machine, not including Gensets, which is connected via an alternator or inverter to the Main Bus. These
include but are not limited to Synchronous Condensers, BESS, Ultracapacitors, STATCOMs, etc.

Synchronous Condensers are of particular interest as they are included in typical VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant
designs. The Synchronous Condenser(s) in a VoltaGrid Data Center Powe Plants ensure that the Main Bus Voltage
and Frequency can remain within customer tolerances during the erratic load swings created by A.I. Data Centers.

The typical Synchronous Condenser used in VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant designs is the ABB AMS 1400 With
Flywheel which includes a CCP (Local Control Panel and Sync Con Controller). In addition to local, manual, control
interfaces, the CCP hosts a Modbus Server which can is used for remote monitoring and control.

9. MEDIUM VOLTAGE DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM

The MV Distribution System allows a typical VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant to safely aggregate and supply MV
power to customer FCBs and the Site AUX XFMRs. All Synchronous Machines are connected to the Main Bus via a
GCB, GSU, and ICB or directly to the Main Bus via a GCB. Site AUX XFMRs (and therefore Site LV E-Houses) are
connected to the Main Bus via an ICB. Finally, the Customer is connected to the Main Bus via a set of FCBs.

Power System Analysis Reports and Electrical Protection Relay Configuration files shall be generated by VoltaGrid to
ensure that personnel and all MV equipment are adequately protected against electrical faults and Arc Flashes.
Breakers/fuses shall be coordinated such that the risk of a system-wide blackout due to a single electrical fault is
minimized. The MV Voltage Distribution System customer (Master Control System) control interface is exclusively
provided by SEL Electrical Protection Relays (SEL 751, SEL 700G, SEL 787, SEL 735).

9.3. MANUAL BACKUP CONTROL DEVICES

Remote Operation Switches (Chicken Switches) shall be used to manually open/close MV Electrical Distribution
System Breakers. Remote Operation Switches shall be located at the local protection relay panel, or on/in the
associated enclosure. Remote Operation Switches should be installed in a location that allows the operator to
manually control SWGR without opening MV enclosures.

Remote Operation Switches shall control SWGR via one of the following methods in order of preference:
     · Dry contacts connected to digital inputs at the local Electrical Protection Relay. The Electrical Protection
          Relay will include these inputs in its TRIP/CLOSE logic equations.
                o This method allows the electrical protection relay to monitor/record manual operations, and
                     block-close when unsafe conditions are detected.
     · Dry contacts connected directly to Trip/Close coils/electromechanical control devices.
                o This method bypasses the electrical protection relays, preventing them from monitoring manual
                     operations, and relies on the operator to determine whether a manual close operation is safe.

Rackable SWGR shall be operated by hand (via the Racking bolt and a Racking tool). No electronic control signals are
required, but Truck-Operated-Contacts should be connected to a digital Input at the electrical protection relay ­
allowing the Racked In/Out status to be remotely monitored (at the Control Room HMI).

Each breaker shall include manual open and close pushbuttons. These pushbuttons shall provide a dry contact
connected directly to the trip/close coils. No IED signals/connections are required.

10. LOW VOLTAGE DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM

The LV Distribution System allows a typical VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant to safely aggregate and supply LV
power to all BOP loads, the Ops Building, the WIC, Site Lighting, the maintenance yard, MV Genset Blackstart
Connections, Sync Con E-Houses, etc. The primary LV bus is the main 480V bus in the Site LV E-House, although other
large 480V busses may be found in the system (such as Sync Con E-House MCCs 1, 2, and 3). the main 480V bus in
the Site LV E-House is normally energized by the Site AUX XFMRs but, in the event of a system-wide blackout, it may
also be energized by the LV Backup Genset.

Note: MV Genset AUX systems/LV power is separate from the Site LV E-House distribution system. Each MV Genset
Package or its associated GCB contains a dedicated 13.8kV/480V Genset AUX XFMR which is connected on the Main
Bus side of the GCB. This allows a small group of MV gensets to energize the AUX systems in all MV gensets for which
the associated ICB is closed.

Power System Analysis Reports, Electrical Protection Relay Configuration files, and LV Breaker Settings shall be
generated by VoltaGrid to ensure that personnel and all LV equipment are adequately protected against electrical
faults and Arc Flashes. Breakers/fuses shall be coordinated such that the risk of a system-wide blackout due to a
single electrical fault is minimized. The LV Voltage Distribution System customer (Master Control System) control
interface is exclusively provided by SEL Electrical Protection Relays (SEL 751, SEL 700G, SEL 787, SEL 735) and SEL
RTACs (SEL 2240).

10.3. MANUAL BACKUP CONTROL DEVICES

Remote Operation Switches (Chicken Switches) shall be used to manually open/close LV Electrical Distribution
System Breakers which are rated greater than or equal to 400A. Remote Operation Switches shall be located at the
local protection relay panel, or on/in the associated enclosure. Remote Operation Switches should be installed in a
location that allows the operator to manually control breakers without opening enclosures or standing directly in
front of 480V SWBDs.

Remote Operation Switches shall control breakers via one of the following methods in order of preference:
     · Dry contacts connected to digital inputs at the local Electrical Protection Relay or RTAC. The Electrical
          Protection Relay/RTAC will include these inputs in its TRIP/CLOSE logic equations.
                o This method allows the electrical protection relay/RTAC to monitor/record manual operations, and
                     block-close when unsafe conditions are detected.
     · Dry contacts connected directly to Trip/Close coils/electromechanical control devices.
                o This method bypasses the electrical protection relays/RTACs, preventing them from monitoring
                     manual operations, and relies on the operator to determine whether a manual close operation is
                     safe.

Rackable breakers shall be operated by hand (via the Racking bolt and a Racking tool). No electronic control signals
are required, but Truck-Operated-Contacts should be connected to a digital Input at the electrical protection
relay/RTAC ­ allowing the Racked In/Out status to be remotely monitored (at the Control Room HMI).

Each breaker shall include manual open and close pushbuttons, handles, or switches. These
pushbuttons/handles/switches shall provide a dry contact connected directly to the trip/close actuator. No IED
signals/connections are required.

15.8. LOCAL AND REMOTE MV GENSET OPERATING MODES

The local MV Genset HMIs and the Control Room HMI provide the Operator with the ability to select operating
modes for each MV Genset. These two sets of selections are described in Section 7.3 Manual Backup Control Devices

The Master Controller should interpret local genset HMI and Control Room HMI Genset operating mode selections
as listed below (in order of priority):

     · Operator Panel Operation Selector = Off
                o Genset is locally Blocked

     · Control Room HMI Mode Selector = Block
                o Genset is Remotely Blocked
                o Master Controller should drive the "Remote Block" digital Output on the Remote I/O Module (SEL
                     2411) high

     · Operator Panel Operation Selector = Man
                o Genset is in local control mode and not available for remote start/stop

     · Operator Panel Synch Selector = Off
                o Genset synchronization is locally Blocked

     · Operator Panel Synch Selector = Man
                o Genset is in local control mode and not available for remote start/stop

     · Operator Panel Request Selector = Off
                o Genset is in local control mode and not available for remote start/stop

     · Operator Panel Request Selector = Off
                o Genset is in local control mode and not available for remote start/stop

     · Operator Panel Operation Selector = Aut, Request Selector = Remote, and Synch Selector = Aut
                o Genset is available for remote start/stop

     · Control Room HMI Mode Selector = Manual
                o Genset Start/Stop from the Control Room HMI is enabled, but automatic starting/stopping for IRM,
                     Party Mode, and Minimum Load management is disabled

     · Control Room HMI Mode Selector = Auto
                o Genset is available for automatic starting/stopping as required for IRM, Party Mode, and Minimum
                     Load management

15.9. SPEED/VOLTAGE CONTROL AND LOAD SHARING

During normal operations, the Master Control System (not necessarily the Master Controller) should modulate the
Voltage and Speed Setpoints at individual MV Genset Controllers and DER Controllers to balance the real and
reactive power loads across all generators and to utilize DERs to minimize voltage/frequency disturbances as much
as possible. If required, Speed and voltage bias signals should be transmitted from the Master Control System to the
individual Genset and/or DER Controllers via an Open Source Ethernet Protocol over redundant OT Networks.

The Genset/DER Controllers and Master Controller shall run all gensets/DERs in a communication-assisted Droop
Load Sharing mode via speed/voltage bias signals from the Master Controller to maintain nominal frequency and
voltage during steady-state operations.

Note: If Prudent Synchronous Condenser CCPs may be configured to run their Excitation Control Systems in Constant
Voltage mode.

Note: The Master Controller may outsource Speed/Voltage Bias Control signal modulation/processing/updates to
an FEP, remote I/O module, or generator protection relay, however it should continue to monitor the Genset's
Frequency/Voltage stability and take corrective actions if erratic behavior is observed.

Note: Extreme care should be taken to ensure Under-Frequency Roll Off, Under Frequency Knee Point, and/or
V/Hz slope settings are consistent/aligned across all Genset and DER controllers to prevent kVAR overloading of
specific units during transient events.

15.11. GENERATOR PRIORITY MANAGEMENT

The Master Controller shall provide soft I/O inputs allowing the Operator to set an independent priority level for
each genset in the plant. The Operator should be able to set each genset to a different priority level or set group of
gensets to the same priority level. For example, if there are 50 gensets in a plant, the Operator should be able to set
them to priority levels 1 through 50 or split them into groups of 5 with 10 priority levels or groups of 10 with 5
priority levels.

These priority levels will be used to determine which genset should be started/stopped in pursuance of the IRM and
Minimum Load Management control algorithms defined in the sections below.

If there are multiple gensets in the same priority level, and the Master controller has to determine which one to
start/stop on another parameter, then one of the gensets with that priority level should be selected based on engine
hours. The unit with the most engine hours should be selected for stops, and the unit with the least engine hours
should be selected for starts.

15.12. INCREMENTAL RESERVE MARGIN MANAGEMENT

During normal operations, the Master Controller should attempt to ensure that a minimum number of gensets are
synchronized to the bus as selected by the Operator in a "Minimum Synchronized Generators" setting. This setting
should be accessible from the Control Room HMI.

Additionally, the Master Controller should attempt to maintain enough generators (rounded up) synchronized to
the bus to support a configurable percentage of the customer load. The percentage should be selected by the
Operator in a "Incremental Reserve Margin" setting. This setting should be accessible from the Control Room HMI.
i.e. if the customer load is 30 MW, the Operate has set IRM to 150%, and the plant's MV Gensets are all 3.3 MW
J620s, then 14 gensets should be synchronized to the bus as shown below:

                                                           3.3 = 13.63  14

If the IRM Management and the Minimum Load Management control algorithms are in conflict, IRM Management
shall take precedence over Minimum Load Management.

The Master Controller may automatically start any generators which are set to "Auto" from the Control Room HMI
in order to satisfy either of the objectives defined above. Whenever the Master Controller deems it necessary to
automatically start a genset, it should start two gensets. If both gensets succeed in synchronizing to the bus, the

provided that the engine shut down would not cause an IRM Management violation.

Automatic Genset starts should always be sent to Genset controllers according to Genset Priority Settings ­
Highest priority (1, 2, 3, etc.) should be started first.

15.13. MINIMUM LOAD MANAGEMENT

During normal operations, the Master Controller should attempt to ensure that all gensets are at minimum load
percentage as selected by the Operator in a "Minimum Genset Load" setting. This setting should be accessible
from the Control Room HMI. If shutting a genset down would not violate the IRM requirements defined above, and
the average genset load is below the Minimum Genset Load setting, then the Master Controller should shut down
the lowest priority Genset which is set to Auto in the Control Room HMI.

If the IRM Management and the Minimum Load Management control algorithms are in conflict, IRM Management
shall take precedence over Minimum Load Management.

If an INNIO J620/J624 DIANE Genset Controller asserts the "Delayed Shutdown/Stop/Trip Request" signal, the
Master Controller should preemptively start gensets as required to maintain IRM requirements.

Automatic Genset stops should always be sent to Genset controllers according to Genset Priority Settings ­ Lowest
priority (100, 99, 98, etc.) should be stopped first.

15.14. PARTY MODE

When the Operator sets the "Party Mode" toggle in the Control Room HMI to "On", the Master Controller should
start all gensets which are enabled for automatic starting as defined in Manual Backup Control Devices and Control
Room Human Machine Interface. At the same time, the Master Controller should disable the IRM and Minimum
Load Management control sequences until Party Mode is turned off.

Note: In the future, Party Mode may be automatically actuated by the Data Center Owner/Operator/Control
System via an interface to the VoltaGrid Cluster.

15.15. FAST DISCONNECT

In the event of a GCB Breaker Failure, as detected by the DIANE (GCB Opening Failure signal) or the SEL 700G (52BF
Relay word bit), the Master Control System should drive the DIANE "Fast Disconnect" digital input high ­
immediately shutting the engine down.

15.16. BLACKOUT RECOVERY AND BLACK START

In the event of a system-wide Blackout, the Master Controller should automatically initiate a Blackout Recovery
control sequence and then, once the Blackout Recovery is complete or exited, allow the Operator to initiate an
Automatic Black Start control sequence. The sequence of operations for Blackout Recovery and Black Start are
shown below in Figure 10 and Figure 11.

If either of these automatic sequences stop due to abnormal conditions or a controls failure, the Control Room
HMI should provide a popup with a description of the issue and buttons allowing the Operator to Continue,
Restart, or Exit the sequence.

Critical AUX Loads are as follows:
     · Sync Con E-House MCC 3
     · Operations Building ATS
     · WIC ATS
     · Site Lighting
     · Fuel Gas Distribution System
     · Bulk Urea Distribution System

These loads are required for the safe shut down of the Synchronous Condenser (if required), situational
awareness, and the safe performance of the Black Start sequence.

The first MV Genset to start after a Blackout should start, and closed into the main bus, under-excited in order to
soft start its GSU and the Synchronous Condenser GSU. Then Genset Controller can do this automatically whenever
the Main Bus is deenergized, or the Master Controller can request this function via remote I/O (SEL 2411) and a
digital input at the Genset Controller.

Once the Main Bus is reenergized, the Master Controller should disable IRM and Minimum Load Management then
automatically start and synchronize a number of gensets selected by the Operator in a "Number of Secondary
Black Start Generators" setting. This setting should be accessible from the Control Room HMI.

Once the Black Start Sequence is complete the Master Controller should reenable IRM and Minimum Load
Management.

15.17. HISTORICAL DATA POINTS AND ALARMS

At any given point in time the Master Controller should make all data available to the OT Network that is required
to completely reconstruct all plant equipment statuses. This data should include but is not limited to:

      · breaker open/close states
      · generator run/stop statuses
      · pressures
      · temperatures
      · levels
      · system power/voltage/current/frequency measurements
      · alarms
      · Master Controller control input/output states
      · Speed/Voltage bias control signal values

All Master Controller Alarms/Alerts, internal variables, control outputs, feedback signal measurements, and other
pertinent data should be provided to the VoltaGrid Message Bus and Control Room HMI via Modbus, or other Open
Source Ethernet Protocol messages on the OT Network at all times.

15.18. FAILURE MODES

Master Controller Failures shall not result in system-wide blackouts.

The system shall include two redundant Master Controllers. The Secondary Master Controller shall monitor the
health of the Primary Master Controller and take control if a Primary Controller health issue is detected.

If both the Primary and Secondary Master Controllers have failed and/or lost power, the system should shift to
voltage and frequency Droop and all closed breakers/switches shall remain closed unless an electrical fault condition
is detected by an electrical protection relay.

The Control Room HMI shall not be dependent on the Master Controller. If the Primary and Secondary Master
Controllers have failed, the operator should be informed via alarm(s) but should still be able to see all relevant
system information.

17. VOLTAGRID CLUSTER

The VoltaGrid Cluster is the on-site Kubernetes cluster that hosts critical Plant Control System applications (the
VoltaGrid Message Bus, Control Room HMI, VoltaGrid Gateway Server, and SWM). Kubernetes provides high-
availability for these containerized applications by running them on redundant compute hardware and
continuously reconciling actual system state with the desired system state.

If a node or container fails, the cluster will reschedule the affected Pods on other available nodes. Network
services and Kubernetes Service objects then distribute traffic to the healthy Pods, restoring application availability
and balancing load across the remaining compute resources.

In plain terms ­ if a single server goes down, the cluster automatically moves the applications that it was running
to another server so that operators notice little or no disruption.

17.2. ALARM/ALERT MANAGEMENT

All Alarms and Alerts, from all communications and control equipment, should be collected and organized into a
uniform format by the VoltaGrid Cluster/Message Bus.

Regardless of how a particular Alarm/Alert is classified/transmitted by the equipment/IED OEM that defined and/or
generated it, the VoltaGrid Message Bus shall categorize them as follows:

     · Alarm (Red) ­ An equipment failure or condition will cause, or has caused, one or more pieces of equipment
          to turn off, deenergize, open (breakers), close (valves), or otherwise become inoperable.
                o A breaker has opened due to a short circuit.
                o An engine has shut down due to high jacket water temperature.
                o A Fuel Gas Distribution System ESDV has opened due to a high pressure condition.
                o An IED or Network Switch is in a failed state and is no longer communicating.

     · Alert (Yellow) ­ The state of the system or a piece of equipment has changed in a way that may be pertinent
          to the Operator.
                o A measurement for a system pressure, temperature, flow rate, etc. has entered an abnormal range
                     but no equipment shutdown is imminent.
                o A feedback signal from an IED is missing but no equipment shutdown is imminent.
                o Ethernet Communication was temporarily lost with an IED or group of IEDs but no equipment
                     shutdown is imminent.

                            Example: An ICB failed to open after an Overcurrent Trip was asserted
                o Level 2 ­ Equipment has been shut down and equipment is likely to have been damaged
                            Example: An Overcurrent Trip was asserted on an electrical protection relay
                o Level 3 ­ Equipment shut down has been initiated as equipment damage was imminent
                            Example: An Undervoltage Trip was asserted on an electrical protection relay

separately from this specification.

Significant efforts should be made to eliminate nuisance Alarms/Alerts. For example, if an SEL 700G temporarily
asserts an undervoltage pickup during an engine start, this should be recognized as normal behavior and no Alarm
or Alert should be presented to the Operator.

17.4. CONTROL ROOM HUMAN MACHINE INTERFACE

VoltaGrid will design a Control Room HMI using Inductive Automation's Ignition platform to satisfy the requirements
outlined in this specification. The Control Room HMI application will be served up by the VoltaGrid Cluster and shall
be accessible locally via a web browser on the local OT Network and remotely via the VoltaGrid Gateway Server.
Efforts will be made to configure the Control Room HMI with Perspective such that it is remotely accessible from cell
phones as well as PCs.

All control outputs from the Control Room HMI shall be initiated and confirmed by an Operator, then sent to the
Master Controller over an Open Source Ethernet Protocol. The Master Controller will execute control requests from
the Operator provided that the plant conditions indicate that the action is safe and valid. If the Master controller is
unable, for any reason, to execute an Operator's request, the Master Controller shall transmit an Alert on the OT
Network via an Open Source Ethernet Protocol message.

All telemetry and alarm tags used in the development of the Control Room HMI shall be populated by the VoltaGrid
Message Bus MQTT Broker.

The Master Controller OEM shall provide a soft I/O interface specification (preferably a Modbus Register Map) for
Control Room HMI Operator Control Request Integration. See Message Bus and Control Room HMI Interface.

Note: All Button/Selector clicks/presses shall require confirmation from the Operator before any Control functions
are executed.

17.5. VOLTAGRID GATEWAY SERVER

The VoltaGrid Gateway Server is the on-site bridge between the OT Network and VoltaGrid's cloud infrastructure. It
provides a secure access point for cloud-based applications to access the VoltaGrid Message Bus. The VoltaGrid
Gateway Server also provides a secure path for remote access to the Control Room HMI, Genset HMIs, Relay
AcSELerator Quickset interfaces, SWM, etc.

The VoltaGrid Gateway server allows remote Operations, Engineering, and Executive personnel to remotely record,
evaluate, and troubleshoot equipment, operations, and plant performance.

17.6. TIME-SERIES TRENDING APPLICATIONS

In addition to the Time-Series Trending Screen provided by the Control Room HMI, the VoltaGrid Cluster shall also
host an instance of SWM for viewing high resolution frequency, voltage, and current data from SEL electrical
protection relays. The VoltaGrid Cluster should retain at least 30 days of data for SWM.

Note: proper installation of SEL 735 Power Metering relay firmware, GNSS antennas, and IRIG-B time synchronization
clocks should allow for 3kHZ sample rate CWM in SWM. This allows for in depth analysis of Harmonics and sub-
transient system voltage/frequency performance.

17.7. FAILURE MODES

The failure modes of the VoltaGrid Cluster are defined by Kubernetes Cluster documentation. In general, a single
server failure (or other VoltaGrid Cluster component failure) shall not result in a loss of Plant functionality,
accessibility, operability, or visibility.

18. TEST REPORTS

The following sections detail the test reports, from OEM and third party organizations, which are required to validate
the Power Plant Control System.

18.1. COMPONENT FACTORY ACCEPTANCE TEST REPORTS

OEMs shall provide FAT Test Reports for all IEDs, Controllers, Relays, RTACs, switches, routers, firewalls, and all other
control and communications equipment prior to shipping them to a VoltaGrid Data Center Power Plant location.

18.2. SYSTEM RTDS SIMULATION REPORT

The Master Controller and/or Electrical Protection Relay OEM(s) shall provide an RTDS Simulation Report to verify
that the Gensets, DERs, and Plant Control System can satisfy the customer's voltage and frequency tolerances based
on the anticipated customer load profile.

18.3. MASTER CONTROLLER HIL FACTORY ACCEPTANCE TEST REPORT

The Master Controller OEM shall provide a HIL FAT Report showcasing the Master Controller's ability to achieve all
stated requirements found in the Master Controller section of this document.

The HIL FAT Report should also show system transient frequency/voltage deviation performance during all control
operations and automatic control sequences defined in the Master Controller section of this document.

18.4. SITE ACCEPTANCE TEST REPORTS

All OEMs, Suppliers, and/or Integrators of any portion of the Plant Control System defined in this document shall
perform on-site commissioning tests defined by a VoltaGrid Plant Commissioning Procedure and provide the results
of those tests to VoltaGrid in the form of a SAT Report.
